85% of AI projects fail in 2026.

Not 15%. Not 30%. Eighty-five.

That number isn't from a hot take. It's Gartner's 2026 AI in the Enterprise survey. MIT Sloan's NANDA study went further: 95% of generative AI pilots deliver zero measurable revenue impact.

If you've already bought a chatbot or AI automation and can't point to a single number that moved, you're not the exception. You're the rule.

Here's why.

The #1 reason AI projects fail isn't the technology. It's that the buyer never defined the baseline before the build started.

Real ROI measurement requires three things, and most AI agencies will let you skip all three:

1. A baseline metric pulled before deployment
2. A control group or clean pre/post window
3. An attribution model that converts AI output into dollars

Forrester's 2026 data tells the whole story: enterprise chatbots with no attribution model retain at 31% over 12 months. The same technology, with an attribution model, retains at 87%.

Same model. Same vendor stack. Same kind of company. The only variable is whether the buyer and the agency agreed on how to measure success before they started.

There's a wave of AI agencies right now charging $5K setups and $3K monthly retainers with no attribution clause. They're the 2020 marketing-agency boom all over again, and they're about to crash for the same reason.

If you're about to sign with an AI vendor, ask them one question: "What specific business metric will this move, and what is our baseline number today?"

If they can't answer with specifics on the first call, you're buying a chatbot that won't survive renewal.
