1/ 85% of AI projects fail in 2026.

Gartner. MIT Sloan. RAND. IBM. Forrester.

Every major research firm has now put a number on it, and the number is brutal.

But here's the part nobody is saying out loud. The reason they fail is not the tech.

2/ The #1 reason AI projects fail isn't the model.

It isn't the prompt.

It isn't even the integration.

It's that nobody, on either side of the deal, defined what success was supposed to look like BEFORE the contract was signed.

3/ If you can't state today's number for the metric the AI is supposed to move, you can't prove it moved.

You can't say "missed calls dropped from 38% to 9%" if you never measured the 38%.

You can't say "saved 22 CSR hours per week" if nobody tracked CSR time.

4/ Forrester 2026 data tells the whole story:

Enterprise chatbots, NO attribution model: 31% retention at 12 months.

Same chatbots, WITH attribution: 87% retention.

Same model. Same vendor. Same kind of company.

The only variable is the measurement plan.

5/ This is why the AI agency crash is coming.

$5K chatbot. $3K/mo retainer. No attribution clause.

At 31% retention, vendors need to replace 69% of their book every year just to stay flat. That's a treadmill that breaks every agency on it.

6/ The AI agencies that survive 2026 will be the ones that diagnose first.

Walk into the business. Pull the baseline. Find the $100K-per-year problem. Then prescribe AI as the solution.

You're not selling a chatbot. You're selling a problem getting fixed.

7/ If you're buying AI, here are 3 questions to ask any vendor on the first call:

a) What specific metric will this move and what is the baseline today?
b) How will we attribute revenue or cost back to the AI?
c) What does failure look like and when do we pull the plug?

8/ Vendors who freeze on question 2 are the vendors who won't be in business in 2027.

Vendors who answer all 3 with specifics are the vendors whose clients renew at 87%.

The questions are not gotcha. They're the table stakes you should have asked the first time.
