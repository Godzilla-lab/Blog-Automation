# Directive: Carousel Hook Playbook

> **Read this BEFORE writing any carousel cover (slide 1).** The cover is the single thing that decides whether a carousel gets seen. On a carousel, the cover image + the first line ARE the hook — if they don't stop the scroll AND force a swipe, nothing else on the carousel matters. This file is the canonical source for cover hooks. `directives/thread_to_carousel.md` step 2 points here.
>
> Default vertical: **law firms (back-office intake / lead-rescue)** — Hexa's locked GTM. The frameworks adapt to any niche (see "Adapt to any niche" at the bottom), but ship law-firm hooks unless told otherwise.

## The one job of a cover

A cover must **open a loop the reader can only close by swiping.** That's it. If the cover states a complete fact, the reader gets the point on slide 1 and scrolls on. The hook must withhold the payoff.

### The 3-second swipe test (run every cover through this)
1. Would a busy law-firm owner **stop** scrolling? (pattern interrupt — text or image)
2. Does it leave a **question unanswered** they need the carousel to answer?
3. Is it about **them / a specific moment**, not a faceless category?
4. Does it promise the answer is **inside** ("here's how / here's what / here's where")?

If any answer is "no," the cover is weak. Rewrite it.

## Why our old covers failed (don't repeat this)

Every old cover was the same template: **`[round stat] + [the pain it names]`, in third person.**

- `42% of law firm leads never hear back.`
- `Self-storage facilities miss 25-60% of inbound calls.`
- `Property managers are losing $45,000-$75,000 per year.`

Four reasons these die:
1. **They close the loop.** The whole point is on slide 1, so there's no reason to swipe.
2. **Stat-blindness.** Opening on a percentage reads as "generic business tip / ad." The scrolling brain is trained to skip it.
3. **Third person = no stakes.** "Law firms," "property managers" — it's about *them*, not *you*. No tension.
4. **Round numbers read as invented**, which triggers skepticism instead of curiosity.

**Rule: never open a cover with a bare round stat.** A number can appear, but only attached to a specific person/moment and an open loop (see frameworks H below).

## The 7 hook frameworks (each opens a loop)

### A. Open loop / micro-story
A specific moment, mid-action, with the outcome withheld.
- Formula: `[specific time/person/scene] + [something happened] + [you never saw it / here's how]`
- "At 5:47 on a Tuesday, a signed case walked out of your firm. You never saw it happen."
- *Why:* a named moment with no resolution. The brain demands the ending.

### B. The named-scenario / concrete instance
Replace the category with one vivid case.
- "A $40,000 injury case called your office. It rang six times. Then they called the firm next door."
- *Why:* one concrete story beats any percentage; the reader pictures it happening to them.

### C. Contrarian / "stop doing X" / "it's not what you think"
Challenge the belief they currently hold.
- "Hiring another paralegal won't fix your intake. This will."
- "Your intake problem has nothing to do with your front desk."
- *Why:* it contradicts their plan, which creates tension they need resolved.

### D. Direct callout (qualify the audience)
Name exactly who should stop.
- "If your firm sends callers to voicemail after 5pm, you're funding your competitor's caseload."
- "Solo and small-firm attorneys: this is the one number quietly capping your revenue."
- *Why:* the right reader feels personally addressed; everyone else self-selects out (cleaner audience signal).

### E. Hidden leak / "you're losing X and don't know it"
A loss they can't see — made specific, not statistical.
- "You'll lose 3 signed cases this month. You'll never know their names. Here's where they go."
- "There's a leak in your firm that never shows up on a report. It happens between 5pm and 9am."
- *Why:* fear of an invisible, ongoing loss + a promise to reveal it.

### F. List with a twist
A numbered promise, but with a hook inside the number.
- "7 calls your firm missed last week. At least one was worth more than your monthly ad spend."
- "5 things happen to a lead after they hit your voicemail. Only one is good for you."
- *Why:* lists promise easy value; the twist adds the open loop.

### G. Question that plants self-doubt
A specific question they can't confidently answer.
- "Quick: what happens when someone calls your firm at 6:14 on a Friday? If you're not sure, that's the problem."
- "Could your firm survive a mystery-shop of its own intake line?"
- *Why:* an unanswerable question about their own business is irresistible.

### H. Number reframe (the ONLY safe way to use a stat)
If you must use a number: make it **odd/precise** (believable), **tie it to a person/moment**, and **leave the consequence open**.
- "Call a new lead in 5 minutes and you're 21x more likely to sign them. Most firms take 3 days."
- "The firm that calls back first signs 79% of the time. Are you ever first?"
- *Why:* the stat earns attention but still opens a "so where do I stand?" loop.

## How the rest of the carousel must back up the hook

A great hook with a flat slide 2 still dies. So:
- **Slide 2 pays off the swipe immediately** — deliver the first piece of the answer, then open the *next* small loop ("but here's the part nobody fixes...").
- **One idea per slide.** Short lines. The reader is skimming.
- **Slides 3-7: the substance** — the cost, the cause, the fix, the proof (a mystery-shop result, a before/after). Specifics, not adjectives.
- **Last slide = CTA, unchanged from what works:** `Comment INTAKE and I'll DM you the Intake Leak Audit.` Keep the proven ManyChat comment→DM keyword. Do not replace it with a soft "this is what we do at Hexa" line.
- **Cover image is half the hook** — a pattern-interrupt visual (a phone screen at 6pm, a missed-call log, a clock), never generic smiling-stock. Source via `execution/search_images.py`.

## Adapt to any niche (when not law firms)

The frameworks are niche-agnostic. To port a hook:
1. Pick the framework (A-H).
2. Swap in the niche's **specific lost moment** (HVAC: an after-hours no-heat call at 9pm; dental: a hygiene gap at 2pm Tuesday; property: a tenant maintenance text at 11pm).
3. Keep it second-person ("your shop," "your practice") and keep the loop open.
4. Run the 3-second swipe test.

Never fall back to `[round stat] + [pain]`. If you catch yourself writing a percentage as the first words, stop and apply framework A, B, or E.

## Learnings
- (2026-06-25) Created after diagnosing 71 reels + 40 carousels that all reused one `[round stat] + [pain]` cover formula and got ~no views/leads. The format (carousel vs reel) was not the root cause; the closed-loop hook + 12-niche scatter were. This playbook fixes the hook; niche-down to law firms fixes the audience signal.
